How Long Does It Take to Study for Security+? A Realistic Timeline for SY0-701 and the New SY0-801

ByteBar · November 20, 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

It depends on where you're starting, and anyone who gives you one number without asking that first is guessing.

If you already work in IT — help desk, desktop support, a junior admin role — most people are ready in about 4 to 8 weeks of steady study, roughly 40 to 80 hours total.

If you have some tech background but haven't worked in IT — you've built PCs, you hold A+ or Network+, you tinker — plan on 8 to 12 weeks.

If you're starting completely cold, with no IT background at all, give yourself 12 to 16 weeks, and don't feel bad about it. You're not just learning an exam. You're learning the field the exam lives in.

Those ranges assume about 10 hours a week. Study half that, and the calendar stretches. Study double, and it compresses — up to a point. Your brain needs sleep between sessions to move things into long-term memory, so cramming 30 hours into a weekend does not equal three steady weeks.

What actually decides your timeline

Three things matter. Everything else is decoration.

Your starting point. Security+ builds on networking and general IT ideas. If you already know what a subnet is and how DNS works, whole sections of the exam are review. If those words are new, you have to learn the foundation and the security layer on top of it. That's not a character flaw. It's just more hours, honestly counted.

Your hours per week. Total study time is what counts, but the calendar matters too. Ten hours a week for eight weeks beats the same 80 hours scattered over eight months, because you stop re-learning what you forgot between sessions. Pick a weekly number you can actually hold on a bad week, not your best week.

How you study. Watching videos feels like studying. Sometimes it is. But the exam doesn't ask you to recognize a lecture — it asks you to answer questions, many of them worded like real situations at work. Time spent answering practice questions and reading why each answer is right or wrong moves you toward ready faster than re-watching material you already half-know. A good rule: by the halfway point of your plan, at least half your study time should be questions, not content.

The exam itself

Here is what you're preparing for, in CompTIA's own terms.

Security+ gives you up to 90 questions in 90 minutes. Some are multiple choice. Some are hands-on simulations of real tasks. The passing score is 750 on a scale that tops out at 900.

There are two versions in play right now. The new exam, SY0-801, launched on November 17, 2026, and it's the current version going forward. The older SY0-701 is still available and still fully valid — but it retires on June 11, 2027, and after that date you can't take it anymore. A certification earned on either version is the same Security+ credential. Which one you should take gets its own section below.

A plan with the numbers worked out

A study plan should follow the exam's own blueprint. SY0-801 weights its five domains like this:

  • General Security Concepts — 16%
  • Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations — 24%
  • Security Architecture — 19%
  • Security Operations — 27%
  • Program Management and Oversight — 14%

Security Operations is the biggest slice of the exam, so it should get the biggest slice of your time. Here's what that looks like in a 10-hour study week, with the math done in the open:

  • General Security Concepts: 16% of 10 hours = 1.6 hours
  • Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations: 24% of 10 hours = 2.4 hours
  • Security Architecture: 19% of 10 hours = 1.9 hours
  • Security Operations: 27% of 10 hours = 2.7 hours
  • Program Management and Oversight: 14% of 10 hours = 1.4 hours

Add them up: 1.6 + 2.4 + 1.9 + 2.7 + 1.4 = 10 hours. The plan spends your time in the same proportions the exam spends its questions.

The 6-week version (60 hours total). This suits someone with IT experience.

  • Weeks 1–4: work through the domains in order, using the weekly split above, and answer practice questions as you finish each topic — not someday, that same session.
  • Over the full plan, that split gives you about 16 hours on Security Operations, 14 on Threats, 11 on Architecture, 10 on General Concepts, and 8 on Program Management. The big domains get big time, on purpose.
  • Week 5: mixed review. Revisit your weakest domain first, then take your first full timed practice exam at the end of the week.
  • Week 6: two more full practice exams on different days, with the hours between them spent only on what those exams exposed.

The 10-week version (100 hours total). This suits someone newer to the field.

  • Weeks 1–7: the domains, one at a time, at the same weights — which over 10 weeks works out to 27 hours of Security Operations, 24 of Threats, 19 of Architecture, 16 of General Concepts, and 14 of Program Management.
  • Weeks 8–9: mixed question sets across all five domains, plus your first full timed practice exams.
  • Week 10: final practice exams and targeted repair, same as week 6 above.

Notice both plans end the same way. However long your runway is, the last stretch belongs to full practice exams and fixing what they find.

When to take practice exams — and what "ready" looks like

Start full, timed practice exams in the final third of your plan. Earlier than that, you mostly measure what you haven't studied yet, which teaches you little and can rattle you for no reason. Short question sets belong in every session from day one; the full 90-minute exam simulation is a finishing tool.

And one good score is not a readiness signal. A single high score can be luck, a familiar set of questions, or a good day. Ready looks like this instead:

  • You score comfortably above passing level on two or three different full sets, on different days — fresh questions each time, never ones you've seen.
  • You finish with time to spare, not racing the clock on the last ten questions.
  • When you miss a question, you can read the explanation and say, in your own words, why the right answer is right.

That last one matters most. The real exam will phrase things in ways no practice set used. Understanding travels. Memorized answers don't.

701 or 801: which one should you take?

Plain terms:

Take SY0-701 if you're already deep into studying for it and nearly ready, or an employer or school specifically requires it. It's valid, it's the same credential, and it stays available until June 11, 2027. Switching exams mid-preparation wastes the hours you've already banked.

Take SY0-801 if you're starting now. It's the current exam, built on the current objectives, and your preparation won't be aimed at a version with a retirement date on it. There's no prize for learning the older blueprint when you're beginning from zero.

Either way, don't let the choice stall you. A week spent deciding is a week not spent on Security Operations — and that domain is 27% of your exam.

One last thing

Whatever timeline you land on, write it down as a weekly plan with real hours in it, the way the plans above are built. "Studying for Security+" as a vague intention can drift for months. Ten scheduled hours a week, divided by the blueprint, finishes on a date you can point to.
